# The NoiseBox Manifesto

Now, more than ever, humanity needs NoiseBox. 

You want to be safe online. You have set all the recommended privacy settings on your computer. You’ve configured firewall rules, disabled location services, restricted app permissions, and managing browser cookie blocks. But this only protects you from incoming threats. Today the biggest threat we face is from what goes out of our computers.

Once your data leaves your computer, it flows through a vast ecosystem of third-party trackers, analytics scripts, and advertising networks embedded across the websites you visit. This data is intercepted, logged, and aggregated into detailed behavioral profiles that map your habits, interests, and device characteristics. Commercial data brokers then harvest, package, and sell these profiles to advertisers, corporations, and other entities (such as governments) for targeted marketing, risk assessment, and automated tracking.

The downstream effect of this pervasive data collection impacts people in deeply intrusive ways. Insurance quotes, travel bookings, or online retail prices suddenly increase based on harvested profiling data that suggests an urgent need or a higher willingness to pay. Advertisers prey on private vulnerabilities, such as spamming relentless promotional material for medical conditions, financial distress, or addictive products just from a person’s moment of casual research. Beyond consumer manipulation, individuals frequently discover that their personal preferences, political leanings, and private behavioral histories have been packaged and sold without their explicit consent, turning their daily routines into commodities traded quietly among unseen commercial entities.

Law enforcement and other governmental agencies are bound to respect personal privacy by the U.S. Constitution’s Fourth Amendment. But corporations are not. More and more, government agencies are “partnering” with private firms to obtain complete profiles of people’s interests, activities, and beliefs that they could not get legally themselves.

Artificial intelligence has fundamentally transformed how corporations harvest and synthesize digital footprints, enabling them to assemble hyper-detailed psychological and behavioral profiles of individuals at an unprecedented scale. Rather than relying on simple metadata or isolated data points, modern AI systems seamlessly aggregate disparate streams—ranging from browsing habits and geolocation trails to social media interactions and purchasing patterns—to uncover hidden connections and predict personal habits. 

By continuously analyzing these vast datasets through advanced machine learning models, automated profiling engines can deduce intimate characteristics like political preferences, emotional states, and private vulnerabilities without requiring explicit user disclosure. This capability turns everyday online activity into a permanent, highly commercialized digital identity, giving corporations deep leverage to influence human behavior, automate decisions, and monetize personal lives without meaningful consent.

So what can you do? How can you fight back?

The answer is surprisingly simple. Pollute the data stream coming out of your router. That is what NoiseBox is for. While you go about your normal online business, and even while you are away from your computer, NoiseBox runs silently, safely, and unpredictably in the background, piggybacking your real browsing footprint with HTTP requests, making connections, grabbing links, and exploring one site after another. Sites that are totally unrelated to anything you normally visit. It uses its own variable wake and sleep cycles to mimic real human web surfing.

The data that flows upstream to your ISP and on to the labyrinth of data harvesting engines now has no discernible pattern; no predictable footprint that allows any inferences to be made about who you are. NoiseBox is thousands of digital footprints walking all over yours in all directions. NoiseBox is that small stream of toxins that ultimately pollutes an entire river. 

And since it sends its continuous output through the router that you, your family, and anyone else with a device connected to it use, it is doing its job for everyone who is online.

With NoiseBox, one person can fight the pervasive and growing data harvesting juggernaut. One person can make a difference; take a stand for their own personal privacy. Imagine what a stand a hundred, a thousand can make to the soulless march of Big Data.
